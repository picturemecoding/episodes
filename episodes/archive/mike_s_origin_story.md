### High School

There’s a Wes Anderson film, *Asteroid City*, where high school kids come to the desert city somewhere in the Southwest for a conference of Junior Stargazers. They’re all geniuses, which seems to happen a lot to kids in Wes Anderson films. But this is something that actually happened in your life, right?

I’ve heard other people talk about copying code from magazines (maybe related: I’ve written down what I’ve seen in books). What was the appeal to writing programs in the early days if you can describe it?

### Supercomputer Center

Sounds to me like Cliff Stoll’s workplace in Cuckoo’s Egg. These are scientists, not necessarily people who would think of themselves as programmers? Code was a means to an end for the scientists?

I walked by the Supercomputer center on the UCSD campus one day and I was like, there it is! I wanted to go inside and see the cray and sit on those blood-red vinyl couches that the Cray has arrayed around it.

Can you call any of these people up who you met and we can try to get them on the show? I think I’d probably call Jaron Lanier first.

### 1992: Professional Software Building

This is pre web-dev so deploying on workstations, but what practices from then persisted into today? What would look the same to somebody 30 years later?

### 1997

Design patterns: you have this line, “Eventually people came to their senses and realized that patterns are not things you invent but things that are revealed by their frequent appearance.”

This makes sense but that fact is something that bothers me about design patterns: they’re just some things that have appeared in the past. When you start to assume they have *primacy*, then it goes off the rails. It’s like saying, “this is the way we’ve always done it,” and I don’t find that very satisfying. Contrast with, “there are laws that these objects follow in their behavior.”

This period makes me think of Office Space: did people dress like that?

### Yahoo!

Tell me about how a recommender system works.

How did you handle scale at Yahoo back then: did you use CDNs? Did you have to deploy your code on different machines around the world? Did you have like coordinated rollouts of application code and database migrations?

I’d like to know more about solving problems at scale: what was eye-opening about that?

Do you remember what it was like interviewing at Yahoo? (Your company was acquired, but did you participate in interviewing other people: did they ask stuff like how many golf balls you can fit in a 747?)

Did people give you credence after Yahoo! because you’d worked at a large internet concern? Did it give you any currency at later jobs? (I don’t remember you bringing it up a lot: sometimes I’ve worked with people and it seemed like they were constantly saying, “At Amazon, we…” and it was used to give weight to what they were saying, but I’ve never heard you do anything like that…)

### 2009-Present

This is the period when I met you. We became friends roughly ten years ago!

Serious question: when I started everyone was young. Sometimes it felt like there was a lack of maturity in these orgs and even sometimes a kind of pride in this like people were deliberately not grown-ups engaging in super grown-up behavior. I remember having the feeling around the time I met you, like, “where are all the elders?” In other words, where are the people who have seen some shit who can tell us what we’re doing wrong? Where did all the people with your tenure go?

You called this the fintech phase of your career. I didn’t have phases like that. I had the first part where I was kind of crazy (from celiac and lack of sleep), and then the part where I was anxious all the time, then the nerdy part with you and a bunch of academics, then my “big company” phase, and finally my “I’m going to build everything at this company from scratch" phase. What’s my next phase going to be?
